# ADR 0007 — Editorial voice must demonstrate engineering mastery

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-08-08

## Context

PCam sells engineering solutions for tool, mould and high-mix precision manufacturing. Prospects must trust that PCam understands the production system, machines, workflows, tooling, scheduling, measurement and automation constraints — not merely that it sells software and robots.

The legacy site contains significant domain knowledge but often presents it as product lists or generic marketing language.

## Decision

Editorial content must demonstrate domain knowledge through precise explanation, operational context and evidence.

Preferred structure for solution/product/application pages:

1. manufacturing problem or constraint;
2. why the problem occurs in real production;
3. engineering approach;
4. how PCam components interact;
5. interfaces/constraints/specifications that matter;
6. operational outcome;
7. evidence or relevant installation;
8. clear next action.

## Voice

Use clear professional English aimed at engineers, manufacturing managers, toolmakers and technical decision makers.

Prefer:

- concrete manufacturing terminology;
- cause/effect explanations;
- real workflows;
- integration details;
- diagrams, real images and videos;
- measured or source-backed results.

Avoid:

- empty superlatives;
- buzzword density;
- unexplained acronyms;
- claims that could apply to any industrial software vendor;
- fabricated precision or invented ROI numbers.

A page may be commercially persuasive without becoming vague. Technical depth is part of the sales strategy.

## Consequences

Some pages will be longer than generic marketing pages, but they must remain scannable. Engineering detail should be structured, not dumped into unprioritized feature lists.
